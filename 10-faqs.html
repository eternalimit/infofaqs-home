BUSINESS & PARTNERSHIPS

10 FAQs for Partners

Before We Meet. Tell the partners what's on your mind.

1. What exactly are we building?

We're building a business that solves real problems for real people. Your Company Incorporated is the company. InfoFAQs.com is one of the places where we put that business to work.

2. Who is our customer?

Someone with a problem worth solving. We need to know who they are, what they need, and why they'd choose us.

3. How do we make money?

By delivering something people are willing to pay for. Not by counting visitors, making promises, or talking about what we might do someday. Revenue matters.

4. What makes us different?

We listen first. We ask better questions. We give people straight answers. And when we say we're going to do something, we follow through.

5. Where do we stand financially?

That's a question for the books. I want the actual numbers. Revenue, expenses, cash, obligations, and what's left over. No estimates dressed up as facts.

6. Who owns what, and who's responsible for what?

Put it in writing. Ownership, authority, responsibilities, and compensation. A handshake is a beginning, not a substitute for a clear agreement.

7. What's working right now?

Whatever we can demonstrate. A working product, a paying customer, a completed transaction, or a verified result. If we can't show it, we shouldn't call it finished.

8. What's holding us back?

Unanswered questions, unfinished work, and decisions nobody wants to make. Let's identify the obstacles instead of talking around them.

9. What happens in the next 30 days?

We agree on three priorities, assign an owner to each one, set deadlines, and measure results. Then we meet again and see what actually got done.

10. Why should we trust each other?

Because we tell the truth, even when the answer isn't what somebody wants to hear. We keep our word. We admit mistakes. We correct them. And we don't confuse friendship with accountability.

Charles, those are my ten. Now I want to hear yours.

Tell me where you agree, where you disagree, and what I'm missing.

I don't want ten people nodding their heads. I want partners who think, challenge each other, and make decisions.

Let's get to work.

Richard Stein
Founder, Your Company Incorporated
